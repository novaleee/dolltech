Software Recommendations

Generally speaking, what we're looking for are pieces of software that function as alternatives to software produced by large tech corporations, which often having a business model that revolves around surveillance capitalism. The ideal for an alternative software is one that is Free and Open Source (FOSS), popular enough to be widely-used, subject to security audits , and is created with a privacy focus in mind. Not every piece of software is going to fit all four of these requirements, which is okay! We're looking for the best possible fit we can find, knowing that something that matches at least one or two of these requirements is going to be a big improvement over surveillance capitalism tech.


Authentication

Passwords

Make sure to use long, unique, randomly generated passwords. Ideally these are generated with a password manager. The basic idea behind a password manager is that one uses a piece of software that keeps all of your login information in one place, ideally where it is secure and encrypted. You access this information with a master password, which is the only password you need to remember. This allows for the creation of long strings of random characters, which are significantly harder to be guessed or hacked.

Recommended Software

  • Bitwarden
  • KeePassXC
  • 1Password

Two-factor Authentication

Two-factor authetication (2FA) creates a second level of protection between your data and anyone who wants to access it. The default for many websites and services is to use a text message based authentication code (via SMS). While this is okay it isn't ideal, due to the inherently insercure nature of SMS technology. A better solution is to use a 2FA app on your phone. You will connect this to whatever account you need to add 2FA to (ideally all of them!). Once connected, when you go to login to that account with your username and password, you will then be prompted to enter a 2FA code or token. You then go to the app on your phone and look for the unique code that is connected to that account (notice that the codes reset every 30 seconds). Enter the code into the website you're trying to login to and you'll be granted access.

Recommended Software

  • Aegis Authenticator (Android only)
  • Tofu (iOS only)
  • 2FAS (Android or iOS)


Email

It's worth thinking of email as the digital equivalent of a postcard; fine for messages that you're not concerned that others may see, but not fine for anything you want to attempt to keep private! For those who work in some government or public service capacities, such as librarians, email can be subject to FOIA and thus may be viewable by the public. For this reason alone its really important to keep work and personal emails separate, and to take extra care with emails sent through work channels.

Ideally an email provider offers end-to-end encryption (E2E). This means that only you and the intended recipient are able to view the contents of your message. If you are sending via one E2E email provider, such as a Proton Mail address, to another, such as a Mailbox address, the contents are automatically encrypted and are not visible even by the mail provider. There is a caveat here, if you send an email from, say, a Proton mail account to a non-E2E email provider, such as a Gmail account, the contents could be visible by the non-E2E provider (in this case Gmail). One way around this in Proton Mail is to create a Password Protected Email and then send the recipient the password via a secure channel (such as Signal).

Recommended Software

  • Proton Mail
  • Mailbox


Browsers

Ideally you want to use Firefox, or one of it's derivatives. It's free and open source and has at least a modicum of privacy-forward features built into it. If you're using basic Firefox it's recommended to find an article on "hardening" it (basically turning on better privacy protections), such as this one. Alternatively you could use a pre-hardened derivative of Firefox, such as Arkenfox or Librewolf. Note that features of some websites may not work with Librewolf, due to locked down security features. This is where compartmentalization can come in handy, such as in a setup where you have one more open browser (Firefox) for less-secure browsing and another (Librewolf) for more secure browsing.

Recommended Software

  • Firefox
  • Librewolf
